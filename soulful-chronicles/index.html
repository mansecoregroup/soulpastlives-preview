A book by Carl Dourish

Soulful Chronicles

Accounts of Past Lives and Spiritual Realms

Years of regression sessions, the stories people brought back, and the questions they raised. Honest about what cannot be proved, it never tells you what to believe.

Order the bookPaperback, on Lulu

Have you ever lived before?

A woman who has always felt she belongs to the sea. A man haunted for years by the same dream. A fear of water that no one can explain. Soulful Chronicles gathers the experiences people have described in regression, and what they made of them.

Alongside them, it follows the long history of reincarnation, the research into children who remember previous lives, and the strongest arguments of the sceptics.

Why I wrote it.

For years people told me I should write about what I was seeing in sessions. For years I found reasons not to. The biggest was fear: of what people would think, and of putting something so personal into print.

In the end I realised I didn’t need all the answers. I only needed to share what I had experienced, what other people had reported, and the questions it had raised for me.

What’s inside.

Past lives
The experiences people describe in regression, and what they made of them.
The time between lives
Accounts of what seems to come after one life and before the next. Between-lives regression
Soul connections
Relationships that seem to reach across lifetimes, and the people we feel we already know.
Fears and feelings
Phobias and emotions with no obvious origin, and what emerged when people looked further back.
An ancient idea
Reincarnation across cultures and centuries, and the pioneers of regression.
The evidence, and the doubt
What the research suggests, and the strongest arguments of the sceptics.

Where it began for me.

I didn’t set out to find a past life. I was trying to understand a fear of presenting my own work, one I couldn’t trace to anything in this life. So I did what I would do with a client: I relaxed into self-hypnosis and asked to be taken to the earliest memory behind it.

I expected something from childhood. Instead I found myself as a middle-aged man in seventeenth-century London, with three children who saw me as their father. My first reaction wasn’t certainty. It was, how on earth did that happen?

That question, and later the loss of our son James, shaped everything in the book.

I am not asking you to accept everything in these pages as fact. I am inviting you to consider the experiences, the patterns that emerged and the effect they had on the people involved.
